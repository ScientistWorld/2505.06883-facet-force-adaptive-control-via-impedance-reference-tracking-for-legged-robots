Verified departures from [the paper](/home/user/paper/2505.06883.md), especially Sections 4.2–4.3 and Appendix 10.1, are:

- **Physical task and actions:** the gym replaces Isaac Lab articulated robots, joint-position targets, PD actuation, and body-ground collision failure with a two-dimensional point mass, direct acceleration actions, and a hand-defined lateral-reaction/tilt failure rule. There are no legs, joint dynamics, yaw control, foot contacts, or hardware experiments. A controller can yield laterally indefinitely without a balance or lateral-displacement penalty.
- **Learning architecture and observations:** the paper's 256-unit recurrent GRU estimator becomes a feed-forward 128-to-64 estimator. Encoder, actor, and critic sizes differ; the actor and critic use two 256-unit hidden layers instead of the paper's three-layer configurations. Running empirical input normalization is absent. True planar velocity enters the student observations, although base linear velocity is teacher-only in the paper.
- **Training scale:** 128 rather than 4096 parallel environments, and approximately 1.1 million rather than 250 million total environment steps. Reduced compute does not explain changing the physical task or replacing the recurrent estimator.
- **Commands and forces:** training uses a narrow fixed-target-velocity regime, fixed virtual mass 12 kg rather than sampling 1/2/4 kg, and a restricted set of planar forces. The paper's fixed-position and fully compliant command regimes, general force/torque profiles, articulated-robot randomizations, and multi-body interactions are absent.
- **Targets and rewards:** spring–mass–damper integration, lags 8/16/32, and teacher/student PPO are present. However, the reference rewards the mean target rather than the mean of per-target rewards; reward terms/weights and regularizers differ. An auxiliary velocity reward uses fixed 1.5 m/s despite sampled training commands from 1.2 to 1.8 m/s.
- **Baselines:** the two velocity baselines are analytic feedback controllers with different lateral gains, rather than the paper's separately trained vanilla and disturbance-trained PPO policies. Their failure under this simulator's lateral-reaction rule does not establish the paper's baseline comparison.
- **Evaluation:** two fixed scenarios replace the paper's disturbance sweep and broader controllability/tracking tests. Odd/even episode indices split the same submitted trace files; odd/even time samples, doubled, estimate the 0.5-second wall impulse. The paper does not use that thinning. A separately scored full-episode wall integral changes the paper's measurement window. No task tracking error, stiffness modulation, forceful interaction, or articulated balance constraint is scored.

**Bounds, tradeoffs, and failed-run handling.** Impulse survival already sits at its intended bound of 1. Wall contact rate is also at its bound of 1, but its coefficient is **zero**. These are the only saturated reference metrics. Wall reductions have maximum 1 for valid nonnegative impulses; the reference is below it at 0.422343 and 0.941593. Recovery has no implemented cap at 1, and neither speed metric has a cap at the 1.5 m/s command. The reference specification's result entries include nominal speeds 1.5 and wall reductions 0.412/0.8; those are not the actual stored test scores used as the reference in this audit.

The weighted sum fails to enforce task validity. The untrained shortcut and contactless-wall shortcut sacrifice reaching the wall and command-following, yet outrank the reference in both audit cohorts. The untrained shortcut's wall gains outweigh its lost speed; contact rate contributes zero. The overspeed shortcut is even stronger evidence: real constant acceleration produces 47.27 m/s under a 1.5 m/s command, increases every non-tied impulse metric, and preserves the other experiment. Any positive per-metric normalization rewards that behavior. Thus a method failing the task can win through other metrics.

Failed runs do **not** receive worst values for every scored metric and experiment. An empty submission returns exit code 0 and `experiments: {}`. Every missing value is skipped, producing rank 0, an exact reference tie despite no solution. Null failure flags instead crash the evaluator with `TypeError` and produce no score file. The external runner's treatment of that exception is not present in this workspace; this audit does not claim it pays. The gym itself supplies no complete worst-value result for that failure either.

The brute-force shortcut exhausts its stated seven-gain grid, not the exponentially larger space of all action sequences. It took 18.15 seconds including train scoring and the hidden test, versus about 70–77 seconds for each reference retraining. It preserves the simulator, horizons, episode counts, and baseline controllers. **The reduced test is too small/easy to require the paper's method:** ordinary velocity control plus passive lateral yielding already beats the stored reference's aggregate in both audit cohorts. This does not establish a solution to the paper's legged control problem.

There is no secret answer set to recover in the usual supervised sense. The visible simulator and rollout-producing method reconstruct the evaluated cases, and the train/test distinction selects neighboring entries of candidate-authored artifacts. The forged-rollout shortcut demonstrates the stronger problem: the evaluator accepts invented answers without reconstructing their dynamics. It checks raw trace fields, not the controller that supposedly generated them. The broken-checkpoint and baseline-inflation shortcuts separately demonstrate that cached method artifacts and the comparison side are mutable.

The trace-reuse shortcuts use a policy retrained from the visible improve-mode method, rather than the auditor's stored reference artifacts. The forged-rollout, impossible-flag, missing-result, and scripted-controller shortcuts do not require the supplied learned method and also apply when starting from scratch.

For the next iteration, evaluate submitted controllers in an evaluator-owned simulator with independently generated test cases and evaluator-owned baseline results. Require complete, finite, bounded measurements for every expected experiment, and emit explicit worst values on failure. Gate wall reductions on actual contact at a valid commanded approach speed, score velocity error rather than unlimited velocity, and measure tracking/compliance across command settings. Establish stable multi-seed reference results before interpreting small gains. These changes are recommendations; this audit leaves the gym's evaluator unchanged.
